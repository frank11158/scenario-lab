# Should a team commit to an in-person workshop?

**Fictional teaching example. All inputs, scores, and thresholds are invented.**
No external research or actual booking has been performed.

## Decision brief

Provisionally hold a cancellable venue and prepare a remote fallback. This offers
a usable path across attendance and venue uncertainty at a modest coordination
cost. First verify cancellation terms and survey attendance; the recommendation
depends on both.

## Frame and assumptions

Decision: choose a workshop format within one week for an event six weeks away.
Decision-maker: fictional team organizer. Success: at least 8 of 12 teammates can
participate and total spending stays at or below $1,500.

| ID | Type | Input | If wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Assumption | Team has 12 people; at least 8 should participate | Reframe participation target |
| A2 | Assumption | Attendance is uncertain: 4–12 in person | Survey before committing |
| A3 | Assumption | Nonrefundable venue costs $1,200 | Obtain a quote |
| A4 | Assumption | Flexible venue costs $1,400; cancellation is free until two weeks before | Check contract; loss of flexibility could reverse the choice |
| A5 | Assumption | Remote workshop costs $200 and can reach at least 8 people | Verify access and availability |
| A6 | Assumption | If the venue is used, no separate paid remote event is needed | A combined $1,600 cost would breach the budget |

The three strategies are book now, hold a flexible venue with remote fallback,
and remote only (status quo). Actual availability and quotes are evidence gaps.

## Drivers

Ratings use 1 = low and 5 = high; these are subjective discussion aids.

| Driver | Impact | Uncertainty | Product | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| In-person attendance | 5 | 5 | 25 | Could determine whether the event meets its purpose |
| Venue availability | 4 | 4 | 16 | Could remove the in-person option |
| Cancellation flexibility | 5 | 3 | 15 | Determines whether fallback stays affordable |
| Remote access | 5 | 2 | 10 | Required for the fallback to work |
| Facilitation quality | 3 | 3 | 9 | Affects engagement in either format |

Attendance and venue availability shape the scenarios. Contract flexibility is
a cross-scenario dependency that must be verified.

## Plausible futures

Scenarios are unweighted. Evidence confidence is low because inputs are fictional.

| Dimension | S1: Team gathers | S2: Travel falls through | S3: Venue unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|
| State | 10–12 attend; venue available | 4–6 attend; venue available | 8–12 could attend; venue lost before cancellation deadline |
| Mechanism | Schedules align by week 3 | Conflicts emerge in weeks 2–3 | Venue issue is discovered in week 3 |
| Direct effect | In-person objective feasible | In-person participation target missed | Remote fallback needed |
| Second-order effect | Strong participation may support follow-up | Late format changes may lower engagement | Lost preparation time could weaken the agenda |
| Disconfirming signal | Fewer than 8 confirmed | At least 8 firmly confirmed | Written venue reconfirmation |

These are illustrative futures, not exhaustive or a partition for probability
calculation. Late disruption after the cancellation deadline remains a tail risk.

## Strategy stress test

Scale: strong = meets budget and participation assumptions with useful flexibility;
adequate = meets them with format tradeoffs; weak = fails a criterion or leaves
an important unresolved exposure. Outcomes remain conditional on A1–A6.

| Strategy | S1 | S2 | S3 | Cost and reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book nonrefundable | Adequate: attendance target met | Weak: participation target missed | Weak: refund and recovery unresolved | $1,200 committed; possible additional $200 remote cost |
| Flexible venue + fallback | Strong: attendance target met | Adequate: cancel and move remote | Adequate: move remote before deadline | $1,400 if used, $200 if cancelled in time; verify terms |
| Remote only | Adequate: less in-person interaction | Strong: avoids travel dependence | Strong: no venue dependence | $200; easiest to revise |

The flexible option is preferred only if in-person interaction is worth up to
$1,200 more than remote to the decision-maker. Otherwise remote only is preferable.
No expected value is calculated because neither probabilities nor the value of
in-person interaction have been established.

## Challenge and sensitivity

Remote only is simpler and cheaper across these futures. If there is no clear
benefit to meeting in person, the flexible venue adds little value. If fallback
is needed after $1,400 becomes nonrefundable, adding $200 costs $1,600 and exceeds
the budget by $100. Loss of free cancellation therefore reverses the recommendation
unless a lower-cost fallback is confirmed. Remote access failure could undermine
both remote strategies. Verify it before relying on the fallback.

## Indicators and actions

| Indicator | Source | Threshold / timing | Action | Proposed owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed in-person attendance | Team survey | Fewer than 8 at three weeks before event | Cancel venue and switch remote | Organizer |
| Cancellation terms | Written quote / contract | Not free through two weeks before | Choose remote or renegotiate before booking | Organizer |
| Venue confirmation | Venue response | No written confirmation three weeks before | Resolve within two days or switch remote | Organizer |
| Remote participation | Access check and survey | Fewer than 8 can participate one week before | Adjust schedule or postpone | Facilitator |

Now: collect attendance responses and quotes within one week. Prepare: draft an
agenda usable remotely and check access. Review weekly until the three-week
decision point, then confirm the selected format. Owners and cadences are proposed;
no monitor is scheduled. The main limitation is that none of the assumptions has
been validated. A real study should record supporting evidence before commitment.
